**Schema design (binding; the tests check exactly this):**
- Ids are TEXT, times INTEGER UTC epoch milliseconds. Batches are out of scope here: a batch member will point at its operation (T034f).
- An operation's target depends on its kind: `fulfillment` and `checkout_rejection` name a checkout (and no claim), `test_fulfillment` a claim (and no checkout), and every other kind a subscription (and neither). Only a `checkout_rejection` may name the discrepancy that caused it. Its clocks are never before `accepted_at`, and each can be set once but never changed; the identity columns never change. At most one open (`accepted`, `in_progress` or `manual_hold`) `leak_replacement` and one open `cancellation` per subscription.
- Remote work is one desired state (`action`, `desired_revision`) of one child for one operation. Each network request is a row of `composite_remote_attempts`, numbered 1 to 5 per work. An attempt is `in_flight`, then `applied` or `rejected_no_effect` (a definite answer within its timeout) or `uncertain` (its own `quarantine_until`, later than the moment its connection closed). A decided attempt never changes.
- `confirmed` work needs a matching read-back (`observed_matches` 1) observed after every one of its attempts was dispatched; it is final, and takes no further attempts.
- A sweep is recorded while it runs (no `outcome`, no `ended_at`); its divergences are inserted while it runs; it ends `consistent` only with no divergence and `diverged` only with one; a finished sweep never changes.
- A discrepancy is `descriptor` (a binding, observed panel values) or `repeated_divergence` / `identity` (a child). One open discrepancy per binding, and per child and kind. A descriptor discrepancy closes as `panel_restored` or `no_longer_in_use`, a child one as `owner_resolved`. `composite_discrepancy_descriptors` lists the in-use descriptors of a descriptor discrepancy.
- A divergence of kind `missing` or `identity` is `not_repairable` and links a discrepancy (no silent recreation); a `paused` repair also links one. The same-owner rules use composite foreign keys onto these unique keys: `composite_drift_sweeps (sweep_id, physical_panel_id)`, `composite_children (child_id, physical_panel_id)` (exists), `composite_remote_work (work_id, child_id)`, `composite_discrepancies (discrepancy_id, child_id)` and `(discrepancy_id, kind)`.
