# Migration, maintenance, deployment and recovery contracts

These are proposed changes to affected `database.py`, `backup.py`, `fsm_storage.py`, `main.py`, `bot_manager.py`, bot/web restore callers and `install.sh`/`manage.sh`/`update.sh`, plus `composite/operations.py`/`cli.py`. No source/deployment change or operation is executed by this plan. E-004/E-005 remain required evidence.

## Shared processing control

All affected processes use an external protected installation/restore-epoch control record and POSIX admission lock outside replaceable database/FSM files. Shared permission covers normal dependent transactions/remote dispatch; maintenance exclusive permission prevents new writers/effects. Worker/panel singleton and per-panel mutation locks are distinct from maintenance. Every participant registers incarnation, open DB/FSM handles and in-flight remote scope; old incarnation may not acknowledge a new epoch. No reliance on a per-Database thread lock as global closure.

Proposed `enter_maintenance(reason, expected_epoch) -> MaintenanceReceipt` closes admission, drains bounded active operations, stops bot reconciler/purge/cache/task reopening, API notifier/background writers and public readers for file replacement, then verifies affected processes/handles closed. Failure to stop a writer is a blocking failure, not a warning followed by overwrite. Persist any dispatched indeterminate remote effect; a closed local process is not proof remote request was cancelled. Preserve all original commercial clocks. Backup freezes local writers briefly without restarting panel/Xray or terminating customer sessions; recovery replacement is a separate outage. Measure pause/detection/urgent-work delay before accepting scheduling.

Proposed `leave_maintenance(receipt, validated_state, next_epoch)`: reopen only version-compatible participants; worker starts in reconciliation/hold mode, obsolete leases/purge requests are fenced, independently verified records resume. For snapshot-only capture with no replacement, preserve epoch and remote-state semantics. Unexpected coordinator death leaves maintenance/recovery marker explicit; no process silently restores mutation permission from stale memory.

Local drain/closure is not remote execution completion. Dispatched uncertainty follows RX-01's detect-and-repair contract in [provider](provider.md#remote-execution-and-fencing) ([RD-10](../research.md#rd-10--rx-01-detect-and-repair-with-continuous-reconciliation)): quarantine, reconciling read-back and the continuous sweep, which resumes after maintenance before any "confirmed" claim. A maintenance pause or restore never shortens a quarantine. Safe observations, accepted protective intents, original cancellation/deadline decisions and otherwise processable local refunds continue; remote uncertainty neither resets a clock nor silently delays credit. A snapshot includes unresolved requests and their quarantine state, not an assertion that they stopped.

## Notification delivery policy N-01

**Proposed engineering settings, unmeasured.** This shared policy covers fulfillment/failure/support/move/owner alerts and optional paid reminders. Retain one logical notice key plus recipient; paid notices additionally bind subscription, successful paid period and type. Queue acceptance is not Telegram acceptance, and Telegram acceptance is not reading. Financial/access/recovery progress never waits for a push unless the separately required initial Telegram retrieval capability itself is unavailable.

**Focused correction, 2026-10-06:** FR-072/FR-079/FR-080 and PC-118 require automatic reevaluation/catch-up for still-relevant, known-unsent notices. The original target is an immutable reporting obligation, not an expiry of delivery eligibility. Separate it from bounded later delivery opportunities; neither waiting nor a restart replenishes the logical notice's finite attempt allowance.

| Control | Proposed rule |
| --- | --- |
| Per call | Verified-peer connection timeout 5 seconds; 15-second total operation timeout including connection, request, response and any supported acceptance lookup. Cancellation/timeout after dispatch is an uncertain send, not proof of rejection. |
| Automatic attempts | At most **five network attempts in total per logical notice**, across its entire automatic lifecycle, including any acceptance-reconciliation calls. Atomically reserve an attempt before network execution; no concurrent send for the same key. Known pre-dispatch transport failures and explicit transient no-send rejections consume an attempt; waiting, local relevance/eligibility checks and queueing do not. After failed attempt numbers 1–4, persist backoff of 5, 15, 30, then 60 seconds respectively; a larger service retry-after sets a later `next_attempt_at`, even beyond the current opportunity. Opportunity changes, blocks, recovery and restarts never reset counts or shorten backoff. Permanent recipient/auth/payload rejection stops automatic sending immediately with the repair need visible. |
| Original targets | Persist immutable `original_due_at` and `original_target_at`, every missed-target event and the originating operation/escalation ID and clock. For reminders, original due time is the first instant verified condition and eligibility both hold, including observation/waiting under FR-080; the normal acceptance target remains due+300 seconds. Required owner alerts are due at their original escalation/recovery-relevant event; display overdue owner attention immediately when safe processing resumes under FR-072. A delivery opportunity is not another 15-minute operational escalation timer or a grace period for credit/access targets. Later acceptance is recorded as late and never erases an original miss. |
| Delivery opportunities and elapsed limits | The initial opportunity ends at original due+300 seconds. A temporary eligibility/known transport block closes that opportunity without exhausting unused attempts. For a still-relevant, known-unsent notice with attempts remaining, automatically open a later opportunity when the block clears, safe processing resumes, or a deferred retry becomes due. Its end is the evidenced renewed-eligibility/processing-ready/retry-due event+300 seconds; record event and detection times, rather than silently using a late worker observation to hide lag. Include scheduling/queueing/backoff and network time in each opportunity, and clip each call to its remaining time. If a window expires with unused attempts, mark its miss and retain `waiting_opportunity`; set `next_opportunity_at = expired_window_end + 30 seconds` and reevaluate automatically. If still ready, that scheduled event opens a replacement 300-second window; it is not a new original due/eligibility event. Do not open parallel windows or shorten a later `next_attempt_at`. No owner action is required merely because 300 seconds passed. Total calendar waiting can span a long restriction/outage while relevant; it is deliberately not a notice-expiry rule. Network work stays bounded by five attempts and at most 75 seconds of aggregate call time, irrespective of the number of local reevaluations/windows. |
| Automatic reevaluation and catch-up | Reevaluate on eligibility/recovery events and at a proposed maximum 30-second scheduler interval under normal processing; use local state, not per-notice network health probes. Immediately on safe restart, rescan pending notices and show overdue required owner attention; queue still-relevant known-unsent owner alerts without waiting for another escalation interval or owner approval. When sending is possible, target starting the next permitted attempt within 30 seconds of processing/eligibility recovery, subject to persisted backoff/service retry-after and the remaining cap. Known unavailable transport leaves durable waiting work for automatic reevaluation. A transient connection/no-send failure sets a timed wait until `next_attempt_at`, not a latch requiring an unrelated successful send: once due and local processing prerequisites are ready, the next counted send can discover transport recovery. An observed outage therefore cannot strand an unused attempt indefinitely. Reminders recheck current terms, enabled type, threshold, renewal eligibility and recipient authority: resume still-relevant notices after release; cancel obsolete term/cancellation/expiry notices without sending. Customer disable alone remains eligible. Five-minute normal reminder acceptance remains measured from each qualifying eligibility event with the original target/miss retained separately. |
| Definitive versus uncertain | `known_unsent` requires no prior dispatch or evidence that every earlier attempt was definitely not accepted and cannot later complete a send. Verified Telegram acceptance/message reference is terminal `accepted` for automatic sending. Lost response, ambiguous service error or crash after dispatch is `uncertain_delivery`, even after an opportunity expires. Reconcile through retained acceptance evidence or a supported read-only lookup if available, subject to the same per-call/300-second opportunity limits and remaining network-attempt cap; a reconciliation opportunity authorizes read-only evidence gathering, not sending; no Telegram lookup/idempotency mechanism is assumed. Unknown-send evidence must establish nonacceptance and exclude a late send before returning to known-unsent. Otherwise retain uncertainty and support visibility; no automatic duplicate-risk resend and no exactly-once guarantee. |
| Persistence and restart | Persist policy version, logical key/recipient/period/type, immutable original clocks and misses, all eligibility/transport-block transitions, opportunity ID/start/end/reason/detection time, global attempt count/history, dispatch/outcome/evidence, `next_attempt_at`, acceptance/uncertainty and exhaustion reason. Persist opportunity/attempt claims transactionally before work. Restart resumes remaining opportunity time or evaluates a new allowed opportunity; it never grants five more attempts. Recorded dispatch without a definite outcome resumes uncertain, not known-unsent. Recheck relevance and acceptance immediately before dispatch. Missing restored attempt/acceptance history requires an evidence hold, not assumed unused allowance. |
| Exhaustion and later recovery | Distinguish `waiting_eligibility`, `waiting_transport`, `waiting_opportunity`, `uncertain_delivery`, permanent failure and actual five-attempt exhaustion. Only elapsed window time is insufficient to declare permanent exhaustion. Actual cap exhaustion or permanent rejection remains visible in owner web with attempt evidence and a repair path; an otherwise permissible recovery attempt with unused allowance is automatic. Accepted notices never rearm. Later evidence can resolve uncertainty/acceptance even after exhaustion. After genuine attempt exhaustion or permanent rejection, an explicit audited owner recovery action for a still-relevant **known-unsent** notice may authorize one 300-second window of at most two additional calls, with 30-second backoff, the same per-call limits, logical key and retained history. Each further such action is separately explicit; no automatic renewal of this manual allowance. Uncertain sends remain excluded from automatic sending unless safe nonacceptance is established. Preserve the existing separate option of an explicitly audited owner-requested send with acknowledgement of possible duplicate delivery; each send while prior delivery remains uncertain requires that explicit acknowledgement and counts against the same two-call/300-second manual allowance. Keep original uncertainty/evidence, and stop further automatic work on any new ambiguous result; neither an opportunity nor a restart authorizes duplicate-risk sending. Notification failures never recursively create new alert notices or stop refunds/access/accounting. |

The five-attempt automatic cap is retained; a separately authorized two-call manual window bounds each existing operator recovery action without making it routine. Five full calls plus the four backoffs consume at most 185 seconds; a 30-second reevaluation allowance leaves 85 seconds of the five-minute normal target for other scheduling delay. These are proposed sizing arithmetic, not measured performance. Separating windows from the lifetime attempt counter permits automatic recovery after arbitrarily long waiting without unlimited network retries. True exhaustion after five actual attempts, permanent recipient/configuration failure or unresolved delivery evidence still requires the existing visible recovery path; elapsed outage/block duration alone does not create an owner task. This repairs the conflict with FR-072/PC-118 without changing product targets or routine operator duties. Holding ambiguous sends preserves uncertainty and avoids automatic duplicates; no new lookup capability is claimed. E-004 must validate scheduling, latency, rate limiting, restart, exhaustion and the recovery scenarios at supported load.

## Additive upgrade

Proposed `inspect_schema` and `migrate(authorized_paths, expected_release)` are versioned, repeat-safe and testable against supported prior schemas. Existing `init_db`/`_migrate_columns` are foundations, not proof. Ship guard/discriminator support before permitting issuance; default existing rows to legacy, preserve users.telegram_id/FSM/defaults/provider meanings and manual/automatic renewal distinction. Main holds composite tables; affected reseller files get only required compatibility/operation metadata, no composite eligibility or automatic data import. Enumerate active/inactive/no-live-bot registry tenants, validate unique paths/identity and owner preservation.

Before migration: identify exact release/runtime/dependency versions, schema ranges/config/key changes; coordinate all writers and capture complete pre-upgrade recovery set. Each DB's migration owns explicit transaction boundaries and does not nest committing helper calls or use a script's implicit commit as atomicity. Multi-file failures retain per-member outcome, leave issuance disabled and restore or repair coherently; no main-only apparent success. Run FK/integrity/schema/index/data/effect/capacity invariants and rerun initialization before reopening. Older binaries must be fenced from active new semantics. No schema drop or invalid old-code rollback over live children.

## Recovery archive

Proposed `capture_recovery_set -> RecoveryPoint` contains main DB (wallet/order/customer/coupon/test/campaign, entitlement/tokens/meter/period/gaps/current+retired identities/work/audit), all explicitly required dependent reseller DBs, affected FSM, release/schema/runtime inventory and machine-readable manifest. Manifest includes installation/capture epoch/time, every expected member and ownership/path identity, checksums, validation results and exclusions. Accept existing `reseller_<id>.db` and historical `reseller_<id>_level<N>.db` mapping, never infer a safe path from archive name alone.

Use SQLite online backup per file under the coherent writer barrier; checkpoint only with appropriate closure, never copy a changing DB while ignoring WAL. Validate integrity/FKs/schema compatibility/domain invariants and missing/corrupt/unexpected/path-traversal/duplicate members in staging. Legacy pre-feature archives without complete manifests need explicit reconstruction/validation and cannot establish no later composite history. They are supported recovery inputs with holds, not automatic safe replacements.

Encrypt archive using versioned authenticated encryption (existing cryptography), store/deliver to owner-authorized off-host SFTP with pinned verified host identity, restricted credentials/access and independently retained keys. No `known_hosts=None`. Completed off-host checksum/validation and capture time establish freshness; transfer attempt/file mtime do not. Candidate cadence5minutes must maintain last verified recoverable capture≤15minutes normally including capture/validation/delivery; measure it. Unknown/overdue copy prompts owner web attention and attempted Telegram alert, without automatic mass suspension/refund.

### Backup delivery policy B-01

**Proposed engineering settings, unmeasured.** Apply to each already captured, validated and encrypted recovery set, with an immutable capture ID/time and archive/manifest hashes. Capture cadence remains five minutes and the last valid recoverable copy must stay within fifteen minutes of its capture under supported normal conditions (FR-076/SC-010).

| Control | Proposed rule |
| --- | --- |
| Per attempt | 10-second verified-host connection/authentication timeout, 30-second no-progress timeout during transfer/verification, and 120-second total attempt timeout including connect, upload, close, publication and verification. Each bound is clipped to remaining total budget. No silent host-key bypass. |
| Attempts/backoff/budget | At most three automatic delivery/reconciliation attempts for a capture, waiting 15 then 45 seconds after retryable failure. Full attempts plus backoff total at most 420 seconds. Overall automatic deadline is capture time + 600 seconds, including capture processing/validation/queueing; if a prior valid off-host point is still within its age objective when this capture starts, also cap it at that point's capture time + 900 seconds. Persist that deadline for this capture. A genuinely new scheduled capture starting after protection is already overdue uses capture+600 seconds to attempt recovery; report the existing miss continuously, without resetting it or reviving an exhausted capture's budget. This preserves the rolling age objective rather than adding ten minutes after a fifteen-minute miss. At deadline record exhaustion/missed protection even if a transfer remains uncertain. No prior valid copy means protection is unknown until verification. |
| Definitive failure | Verified pre-upload connection rejection/no-write failure is retryable only for a transient cause. Host-key mismatch, invalid credentials/permissions, manifest/checksum mismatch or exhausted destination space stops automatic attempts and exposes the exact repair need. Partial files are never recoverable points. |
| Partial/uncertain outcomes | Timeout or connection loss after writes/close/rename may leave partial or complete bytes. Use unique immutable capture-and-attempt paths, a temporary upload and an attempt-specific completion manifest; never overwrite the last valid archive or a shared latest pointer. A supported same-directory atomic rename publishes the closed object, but rename acknowledgement alone is insufficient. Reconnect and reconcile the known attempt path; matching reads alone cannot prove an uncertain writer has stopped. Require close/drain or immutability evidence excluding further changes as well as integrity verification before counting success. If earlier execution cannot be drained, do not reuse, resume, mutate or delete its path; another bounded attempt uses a distinct path so late writes cannot invalidate any validated copy. Orphan/uncertain attempts remain inventoried for safe later cleanup. |
| Completion evidence | Verify remote closed archive size and whole-file checksum against the locally validated encrypted archive and manifest, and verify the completion manifest binds the same members, capture ID/time and hashes. Use an authenticated full read-back/checksum, or a separately validated destination checksum facility; file existence/size or successful SFTP return alone is insufficient. Only immutable, fully verified sets enter the recovery-point inventory; a renamed file with a possibly live writer remains uncertain even if its current checksum matches. Destination rename/closed-file integrity, durable persistence and accessibility after source-host loss require controlled E-004 evidence; an unsupported destination fails binding. |
| Restart and preservation | Persist capture/hash/destination identity, policy version, attempts/paths/dispatch, partial/uncertain state, next attempt, original budget and verified completion evidence. Restart reconciles dispatched paths within remaining budget; it does not assume success or grant fresh attempts. Retain the last valid recoverable copy until a newer capture is independently verified; no failed/late upload deletes or demotes it. Choose the newest verified point by capture time, never completion time. This retry policy authorizes no new retention purge. |
| Exhaustion/recovery | Owner web shows last verified capture time/age, missed age target, pending/uncertain paths, exhausted attempt count and repair action; attempt the existing deduplicated N-01 owner alert independently. Do not suspend/refund customers due to backup failure. A subsequent scheduled coherent capture is a new capture with its own bounded delivery, not a retry relabeled as fresh. Audited later recovery of an old capture may use three attempts with the same per-call/backoffs and a 420-second recovery-window limit; keep the original exhausted budget/miss and immutable capture time. A successful old upload can improve available recovery history but cannot claim current protection when its capture is too old. |

These settings bound connection stalls and leave up to three minutes for capture processing/validation/queueing in the ten-minute candidate window; early completion must supply real margin before the rolling fifteen-minute limit. Full read-back adds bandwidth and temporary attempt files add storage. Measure actual archive sizes, throughput, destination integrity and overlapping five-minute captures under E-004; prioritize newer captures, retain the valid predecessor and prevent delivery from starving urgent work. If supported load cannot meet these budgets, revise the engineering settings within the unchanged target. Additional paid bandwidth/storage, another destination or new routine operator duties require the precise cost/responsibility choice to be presented before adoption; none is assumed or approved here. No operational performance has been demonstrated.

Exclusions: unencrypted environment/decryption/private keys, remote panel/Xray infrastructure, external HTTPS/domain/routing control and machine-wide state. Authoritative DBs may contain authorized tenant secrets and require protected handling. External release/config/key/domain/TLS/prepared-panel recovery inventory is separately maintained; FSM included where needed for observable conversation recovery. Raw samples may compact only with preserved confirmed totals/epochs/allocations/gaps/effect history/tombstones and cleanup responsibility. No hardcoded evidence-erasure horizon or general infrastructure backup service.

## Restore and stale-history reconciliation

Proposed `preflight_restore(archive, external_inventory) -> RestorePreview` decrypts in authorized isolation, validates entire set/member manifests/schema/data/path safety before replacing any main/tenant file, compares current installation/registry/dependencies and reports gaps/skew/missing members. Preserve current complete pre-restore set and newer accessible journals/evidence externally before destructive replacement; include all pre-copy paths in results. Refuse silent partial replacement; if physical replacement is interrupted, remain in maintenance with recovery copy and explicit per-member recovery state.

`restore_validated_set(preview, owner_authorization) -> RecoveryCase` verifies writer stop/closed handles, increments external restore epoch, replaces staged validated set coherently with recoverable checkpoints and cache invalidation, migrates as needed, then starts safe reconciliation before dependent mutations. No stale reconciler/purge can restart writers during replacement. Missing newer files are neither silently retained as a mixed set nor blindly deleted; preview/holds record treatment. Old snapshot/schema alone does not prove current remote identities, money, allowance or campaign capacity.

Reconciliation evaluates preserved newer local evidence and independently verifiable payment references, panel-owned identities/runtime state, token/generation/revocation facts, usage/reset/boundary evidence and coupon/test/campaign outcomes. Hold uncertain effects; never blindly refund/debit/create/enable/reset/reopen claims from snapshot. Pursue safe suspension/cleanup only for verified owned identities, retaining pending native exposure. Snapshot absence is not proof of remote cleanup or no past receipt. There is no zero-history-loss reconstruction promise. Release verified cases independently; widen hold only if affected shared state cannot safely isolate. Usage-only settlement requires intact ownership/purchase/lifecycle/financial/campaign history; other irrecoverable gaps require specific governed remediation/risk acceptance with maintainer/reason/evidence/scope/follow-up, never preapproved generic owner resume.

Report original deadlines/overdue refunds/cleanup/unknown claims/missed targets as soon as safe processing resumes. With beneficiary/amount/prior outcome established, overdue credit becomes spendable within60seconds; otherwise visible evidence hold precedes any repetition. Retain original expiry/grace/cooldown/campaign history and known newer cancellations/rotations. A recovery hold is not confirmed remote cutoff.

Prepared-host recovery target is four elapsed hours from owner initiation **with** prepared host, validated required copy, selected release/runtime/config/authorized keys and functioning external HTTPS/domain/panel prerequisites. Report waiting/procurement/owner response separately; retries never restart clock. Completion evidence includes main Telegram customer and owner web management, existing public URL identities, safe accounting/enforcement/recovery for verified records and truthful held/partial functions. Running processes/copied files are insufficient. No always-ready second host or automatic extension/refund.

## Destructive guard and rollback

Guard in the shared service/database boundary for factory reset, customer/order/subscription/panel/plan removal, tenant tier/delete/ownership/purge and invalidated/stale cleanup requests. Check protected ownership/money/period/usage/token/campaign/remote obligations and all sole dependent management/key references. Return sanitized blocking record/obligation IDs and allowed cancellation/cleanup/equivalent-move guidance. `force`/confirmation cannot bypass. Keeping composite tables while deleting their dependencies is forbidden. Cancelling/refunding every subscription still does not authorize history erasure. Ordinary test reset remains, preserving unresolved holds/permanent campaign recipients. Deactivation excludes new offers/claims, retains existing management/cleanup; it does not automatically revoke access.

Rollback before new issuance may restore tested pre-upgrade state after coherent writer coordination and no new effects. After feature effects, use compatible feature-aware release or validated restoration+newer-evidence reconciliation; old binaries cannot ignore composite work/tokens/refunds and be considered safe. Retain pre-rollback history/cleanup and external keys. Rollback is not a way to reset deadlines/counters/grants/claims.
